**Read this first (method and reliability).**
- About 32 web searches were run. Findings come from search-result summaries; individual pages were NOT opened and read in full. Every figure below should be treated as "reported by the linked source, not independently verified".
- Most company metrics are self-reported (press releases, founder interviews) or come from database aggregators (CB Insights, Dealroom), which often disagree. Conflicts are flagged inline.
- "Tunisian equivalent: none found" means none surfaced in the specific search described; it is not proof of absence. Where no Tunisia-specific search was run, this is stated as "not searched" and listed under Gaps.
- "Adaptations for Tunisia" are the researcher's inferences, not sourced facts, and are placed under Inferences.
- Correction to the brief: Glovo has NOT exited Tunisia (active in 2025–2026, see section 13). Swvl did not die; it shrank, pivoted to B2B and reported a small profit in FY2025.

## 1. B2B retail supply and embedded finance: MaxAB-Wasoko, Chari, Udaan, GudangAda, Cartona — status in 2025–2026 and lessons

### Takeaway
Pure B2B grocery distribution to small shops has not produced a profitable standalone business in any reference market; the survivors (Chari, MaxAB-Wasoko) now treat distribution as a customer-acquisition channel for licensed financial services. Tunisia has one small player, Kamioun, that has raised roughly $0.6M and shows no confirmed 2025 round.

### Cited Findings
**Chari (Morocco)**
- Chari closed a $12M Series A in October 2025, co-led by SPE Capital and Orange Ventures, described as the largest Series A in Morocco; total raised about $17M — [Wamda](https://wamda.com/2025/10/chari-secures-12-million-series-payment-licence-bank-al-maghrib)
- It received a payment institution licence from Bank Al-Maghrib, reported as the first for a venture-backed Moroccan company; the licence covers IBAN issuance, debit cards, transfers and micro-insurance — [Zawya press release](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/chari-closes-12mln-series-a-and-becomes-the-first-vc-backed-startup-to-obtain-a-financial-institution-license-in-morocco-y8b0chhv)
- DisrupTech Ventures joined a Series A extension in November 2025 (amount undisclosed) — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/11/05/chari-raises-funding-from-disruptech-ventures/)

**MaxAB-Wasoko (Egypt, Kenya and others)**
- The merged group wound down e-commerce in Morocco in July 2025 as fintech became its core — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/07/11/maxab-wasoko-winds-down-e-commerce-in-morocco-as-fintech-becomes-core/)
- It obtained an Egyptian fintech licence in July 2025 — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/07/17/with-new-egypt-fintech-license-in-hand-maxab-wasoko-charts-deeper-fintech-led-future/)
- It acquired Egyptian B2B marketplace Fatura in May 2025 — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/05/19/two-exits-one-startup-maxab-wasoko-is-the-latest-to-acquire-egypts-b2b-marketplace-fatura/)
- One outlet reports Egypt fintech operations generating more than $180M a year and e-commerce profitable in three of five markets. This was not corroborated elsewhere and it is unclear whether "$180M" is revenue or transaction volume — [Tech in Africa](https://www.techinafrica.com/maxab-wasoko-bets-on-fintech-over-e-commerce-in-push-for-profitable-growth-across-africa/)
- Wasoko founder Daniel Yu stepped aside as co-CEO in September 2025 — [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2025/09/18/daniel-yu-steps-down-wasoko-ceo-maxab-merger/)
- A February 2026 report describes the group replacing founders with operators in pursuit of profitability, and investor VNV Global marking its stake down in 2025 — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/02/23/maxab-wasoko-swaps-founders-for-operators-in-hunt-for-profitability/)

**Udaan (India)**
- Udaan cut about 350 staff (10%) in November 2022 while targeting profitability — [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/companies/e-commerce-firm-udaan-to-lay-off-350-employees-seeks-profitability-122110401040_1.html)
- It laid off about 150 more days after raising $340M in December 2023 — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/entrepreneurship/story/udaan-lays-off-150-employees-days-after-securing-340-mn-report-409837-2023-12-18)
- A 2024 analysis is titled "Udaan's getting smaller; its problems aren't" — [The CapTable](https://www.the-captable.com/2024/04/udaans-getting-smaller-its-problems-arent)

**Tunisia: Kamioun**
- Kamioun connects neighbourhood shops to suppliers; it reported $400K raised in December 2021 — [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2021/12/10/e-commerce-startup-kamioun-secures-usd-400-k-investment-to-expand/)
- A 2023 article reports about $600K from VCs and angels plus about $100K in grants — [African Manager](https://africanmanager.com/?p=459658)
- An undated La Presse article says Kamioun was trying to raise €900K to expand to Sousse and Sfax and enter Algeria, with a team of 30 — [La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/?p=169844)
- Profile of the company's model — [Mind the Bridge](https://mindthebridge.com/from-trucks-to-tech-how-kamioun-is-reinventing-b2b-retail-in-tunisia/)

### Inferences
- Lesson: the money is in payments, credit and bill-payment commissions distributed through shopkeepers, not in the logistics margin. Both survivors needed a central-bank licence to capture it.
- Tunisia adaptation: with roughly 12 million people, an owned-warehouse-and-truck model has too little volume to cover fixed costs. A lighter version (order aggregation for existing wholesalers, supplier-funded trade credit, bill-payment agency) fits better. A payment-institution licence from the Central Bank of Tunisia would be the hard prerequisite for the Chari path; capital and timeline for that licence were not researched.
- The Morocco exit by MaxAB-Wasoko shows a well-funded foreign entrant could not make Maghreb B2B e-commerce pay on its own.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 information retrieved on GudangAda (Indonesia) or Cartona (Egypt).
- No confirmed 2025–2026 funding, revenue or operating status for Kamioun.
- No audited revenue or profit for Chari or MaxAB-Wasoko.
- Udaan's 2025 financials not retrieved; the "40–45% growth" claim from a July 2025 Financial Express interview appeared only in a search summary and was not sourced to a readable URL.

## 2. SME software in low-ARPU markets and e-invoicing compliance SaaS

### Takeaway
Free bookkeeping apps in India attracted millions of users but still lose money, while compliance software sold after an e-invoicing mandate has a forced buyer. Tunisia's own mandate was extended to service providers on 1 January 2026 and is in a troubled roll-out, which is the clearest "mandate-driven" opening found in this research.

### Cited Findings
**Ledger apps (India)**
- OkCredit narrowed its net loss 34% to ₹23.2 crore in FY25 from ₹35.1 crore in FY24 — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/tiger-global-backed-okcredit-narrows-fy25-loss-by-34-to-inr-23-cr/?login=1)

**Tunisia e-invoicing mandate**
- The 2026 Finance Act extends mandatory e-invoicing to service providers from 1 January 2026, on top of transactions already covered (State, local authorities) — [Business News](https://businessnews.com.tn/2025/12/30/facture-electronique-ce-qui-va-reellement-changer-a-partir-du-1er-janvier-2026/1380856/)
- The system runs on El Fatoora, operated by Tunisie TradeNet (TTN) — [Edicom](https://edicomgroup.com/fr/blog/etat-facture-electronique-tunisie)
- Explainer on the 2026 obligation — [La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/01/07/tout-comprendre-a-la-facture-electronique-qui-simpose-en-tunisie-en-2026/)
- El Fatoora fees: 10 dinars a month per web or EDI account plus 0.190 dinar per invoice (up to 50 KB); the same article is headlined "ready on paper, impossible on the ground" — [Business News](https://businessnews.com.tn/2026/01/05/facture-electronique-une-reforme-prete-sur-le-papier-impossible-sur-le-terrain/1381361/)
- A chartered-accountants' representative pointed to grey areas and application difficulties — [Business News](https://businessnews.com.tn/2026/01/07/faten-baatout-pointe-les-zones-dombre-et-les-difficultes-dapplication-de-la-facture-electronique/1381654/)
- An op-ed calls the reform high-risk for small service providers — [La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/01/04/tribune-facturation-electronique-en-tunisie-une-reforme-a-haut-risque-pour-les-petits-prestataires/)
- TTN membership became electronic in February 2026 — [Managers](https://managers.tn/2026/02/16/ladhesion-a-tunisie-tradenet-est-desormais-electronique/)
- Penalties cited: 100 to 500 dinars per paper invoice issued instead of an e-invoice, capped at 50,000 dinars a year (vendor blog; verify against the law) — [Noqta](https://noqta.tn/en/blog/facturation-electronique-tunisie-loi-penalites-2026)
- A local integrator publishes a guide for connecting ERPs (notably Odoo) to the TTN API — [Noqta](https://www.noqta.tn/fr/blog/integration-api-ttn-el-fatoora-erp-guide-2026)

**Poland (KSeF)**
- Mandatory from 1 February 2026 for businesses above PLN 200M revenue and extended to most taxpayers on 1 April 2026 — [Edicom](https://edicomgroup.com/blog/poland-will-make-b2b-electronic-invoicing-mandatory)
- The second wave brought in hundreds of thousands of businesses — [sharedserviceslink](https://www.sharedserviceslink.com/news/poland-s-ksef-second-wave-expands-e-invoicing-to-hundreds-of-thousands-of-businesses)

### Inferences
- The Tunisian roll-out is messy (postponement requests, missing implementing texts per search summary), so demand for simple, cheap compliance tools and for accountant-channel distribution should be real through 2027. The risk is the State: TTN's own web portal at 10 dinars a month sets a price floor and could absorb the low end.
- Pricing adaptation: TTN's per-invoice fee (0.190 dinar) plus a modest subscription suggests SME willingness to pay is in the tens of dinars a month, not hundreds. This is an inference; no Tunisian SaaS price list was found.
- A ledger-app-then-lending play (Khatabook style) looks weak for Tunisia: it needs tens of millions of users and a lending licence to work, and it still loses money in India.

### Gaps
- No data retrieved on Khatabook revenue, Vyapar, BukuKas/BukuWarung status, Paraşüt/Logo, Daftra/Qoyod/Wafeq pricing, Egypt ETA, Saudi ZATCA, Turkey e-Fatura, Romania e-Factura (SmartBill) or India GST vendors. Adoption curves and price points for these were NOT established.
- No named Tunisian invoicing SaaS with public pricing was found. Whether local vendors already dominate El Fatoora integration is unknown.
- Sources conflict on the Finance Act article number (53 vs 54), and the current state of deadlines or tolerance measures should be checked with TTN or the Ministry of Finance.
- No Tunisia-specific search was run for a Khatabook-style ledger app.

## 3. Fintech adapted to cash economies: BNPL, salary advance, payments, remittance, freelancer payouts

### Takeaway
BNPL has reached Tunisia through banks rather than startups, and salary-backed finance has a funded local startup (PayDay). The clearest unserved need is legal, low-friction payout for freelancers and exporters of services, which is blocked by the 1976 exchange code and awaiting two reforms still not adopted as of the latest sources.

### Cited Findings
**BNPL**
- Tabby raised $160M at a $3.3B valuation in February 2025 — [Tabby](https://tabby.ai/en-AE/press/series-e-funding)
- A secondary sale valued Tabby at $4.5B in October 2025 — [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/10/28/fintech-giant-tabby-hits-4-5bn-valuation-in-share-sale)
- Tabby raised $233M at a $6.5B valuation in September 2026 — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2026/09/tabby-hits-6-5-billion-valuation-233-million-series-f)
- Tunisia: BTK Bank launched "Super Facilité" in June 2025, presented as the first fully digital BNPL in the country, 2 to 12 instalments via the BTK PAY app — [Managers](https://managers.tn/2025/06/16/btk-bank-devoile-son-nouveau-service-super-facilite-une-nouvelle-ere-de-buy-now-pay-later-en-tunisie/)
- Tunisia: overview of who offers BNPL, including UBCI "Flex Pay" via merchant payment terminals — [Webdo](https://www.webdo.tn/fr/actualite/national/achetez-maintenant-payez-plus-tard-qui-propose-ces-services-en-tunisie/379844/)
- Tunisia: in February 2025 startup Ms Solutions was negotiating with local banks to introduce BNPL; no finalised programme existed then — [La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/2025/02/13/buy-now-pay-later-bnpl-en-tunisie-des-negociations-en-cours-pour-son-introduction-avec-des-banques-locales/)

**Salary advance**
- Tunisia: PayDay links insurers, banks and employers to offer salary-backed financing and micro-Takaful; one report says it raised $3M — [Waya](https://waya.media/tunisian-fintech-payday-raises-usd-3m-to-expand-salary-advance-platform/); another describes the same event as a pre-seed round, so the amount is unconfirmed — [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/fr/nouvelles/payday-raises-pre-seed-tunisian-insurtech)
- Tunisia: bank BTE announced a partnership with PayDay — [GPA](https://gpa.net/blogs/africa/tunisia-bte-announces-strategic-partnership-with-payday)

**Payments**
- Konnect (founded 2021, payment links, plugins, API) raised about $1.5M in May 2025 with Attijariwafa Ventures — [Afrique IT News](https://afriqueitnews.com/finance/konnect-fintech-tunisienne-leve-15-m-soutien-dattijariwafa-ventures/)
- Flouci was named in Forbes Middle East's Fintech 50 for 2025 — [Managers](https://managers.tn/2025/03/15/flouci-parmi-les-50-meilleures-fintechs-de-la-region-mena-selon-forbes)
- Cash use in Tunisia is rising even as the fintech ecosystem matures — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=49030)

**Freelancer payouts and FX controls**
- Tunisia moved in December 2025 to scrap a 50-year-old forex rule to retain tech talent and startups — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/12/04/tunisia-scraps-50-year-forex-rule-to-retain-tech-talent-and-startups/)
- A bill tabled in August 2026 would create a legal status for freelancers serving foreign clients, open access to PayPal-type services and impose a levy — [Business News](https://businessnews.com.tn/2026/08/10/freelances-la-loi-qui-veut-ouvrir-paypal-et-preleve-15/1413684/)
- A 15% levy is under discussion in that bill — [Managers](https://managers.tn/2026/08/18/freelances-tunisiens-un-cadre-legal-en-vue-un-prelevement-de-15-en-discussion/)
- Why some international payments are blocked under current rules — [Tunisie Numérique](https://www.tunisienumerique.com/tunisie-paiements-internationaux-pourquoi-certaines-operations-sont-bloquees/)
- A regional guide says Payoneer and Wise withdrawals to Tunisian banks remain limited or uncertain, unlike Morocco — [Mediaterranee](https://www.mediaterranee.com/guides/freelance-depuis-lalgerie-le-maroc-ou-la-tunisie-statut-plateformes-et-paiement-en-devises)

### Inferences
- BNPL as a startup is a poor fit: banks already offer it on their own cards, lending needs a licence, and consumer credit rules apply. A startup can only be the technology or merchant-acquisition layer for a bank.
- A compliant freelancer payout and invoicing service (collect abroad, repatriate legally, handle the levy and tax filing) becomes viable only if the August 2026 bill passes. Being ready on the day it does is the opportunity; building before it does is a regulatory bet.
- A merchant-of-record entity outside Tunisia that invoices foreign clients and pays Tunisian contractors is the Deel/Grey analogue; its legality under current exchange rules was not verified and should be checked by counsel.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Valu and Sympl results, NowPay, Abwab, Refyne, collections businesses, remittance players, Grey and Geegpay status.
- Adoption status of the exchange-code reform and of bill 36/2026 is unconfirmed as of November 2026.
- No user or volume numbers for BTK, UBCI or PayDay products.
- No Tunisia-specific search was run on diaspora remittance startups.

## 4. Healthcare: booking, home care, pharmacy, medical tourism, labs

### Takeaway
Doctor-booking exists in Tunisia (Med.tn) but its paying base is small, and the Egyptian reference (Vezeeta) has lost most of its investor value and is moving into physical operations. Medical tourism is already a large Tunisian sector; no evidence was retrieved on whether its intermediaries are a gap.

### Cited Findings
- Vezeeta said it reached profitability in 2022 when raising growth capital led by Gulf Capital — [Zawya press release](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/vezeeta-reaches-profitability-milestone-and-closes-a-new-growth-capital-funding-round-wkbidbok)

- Yodawy received $10M from Ezdehar (reported January 2024) — [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/egyptian-investment-firm-ezdehar-backs-yodawy-with-10m)
- Chefaa raised $5.25M to expand in Saudi Arabia; sources date this to either December 2023 or March 2025 — [CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Business/Egypt-Based-Healthtech-Chefaa-Secures-5-25-Million-Investment); [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/egypts-chefaa-raises-funding-to-drive-saudi-expansion)

- Tunisia: an official said in October 2026 that more than 500,000 foreign patients are treated in Tunisian hospitals and clinics, led by Libyans, then Algerians, then sub-Saharan Africans — [La Presse](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/10/03/tourisme-medical-plus-de-500-000-patients-etrangers-pris-en-charge-dans-les-etablissements-hospitaliers/)

### Inferences
- Booking marketplaces monetise poorly in small markets: the search summary cited Med.tn figures of 2,000 listed doctors and 700 paying subscribers (2026) from a Managers article whose URL was not captured, so treat as unverified. Even if correct, that is a small revenue base after nine years.
- For medical tourism the patient flow is already there and comes from neighbours paying cash. A facilitator serving European patients (dental, cosmetic, as in Turkey) or structuring Libyan and Algerian flows would earn foreign currency, which sidesteps the small domestic market.
- Pharmacy delivery depends on pharmacy regulation (ownership monopoly, drug price controls, drug shortages); this was not researched for Tunisia.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Practo, Halodoc, Portea, Emoha, lab aggregators; any Tunisian home-care, elder-care, pharmacy-delivery or drug-finder startup. A Tunisian "Cure" was not found.
- Medical-tourism facilitators named in the brief or known informally (Medespoir, Carthagea) did not appear in results; their existence and scale are unverified.
- No sourced 2025 revenue figure for Tunisian medical tourism (a "3.5 billion dinars" figure appeared in a search summary without a clear source).

## 5. Education and emigration: exam prep, bootcamps, talent export, Germany nursing pipelines

### Takeaway
Low-price exam prep works at Indian scale (PhysicsWallah listed in November 2025 and turned a quarterly profit), and Tunisia has already produced its own exportable edtech (GoMyCode). Nurse migration to Germany is organised mostly through public channels in Tunisia with at least one licensed private agency, and a new pilot started in September 2026.

### Cited Findings
- PhysicsWallah's FY25 revenue was about ₹2,887 crore (up roughly 49%) with a net loss of about ₹243 crore — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/09/edtech-physicswallah-hybrid-model-fuels-3000-crore-growth-ipo)
- It listed on 18 November 2025 at ₹145 against an issue price of ₹109 — [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/physicswallah-ipo-listing)
- Q2 FY26 net profit was ₹69.7 crore on revenue of ₹1,051.2 crore — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/physicswallahs-q2-fy26-profit-surge-revenue-growth-trigger-5-stock-upswing)
- GoMyCode (founded in Tunisia) last raised an $8M Series A led by AfricInvest in June 2022 per trackers; funding totals vary by database — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/gomycode)

## 7. Energy and water

### Takeaway
Rooftop and SME solar is growing fast in Tunisia and SMEs struggle to get bank finance, which is the condition under which third-party-financed solar took off elsewhere; no named Tunisian leasing operator was found. Water-purifier subscription has an Indian proof point but its Tunisian status is unverified.

### Cited Findings

- SMEs moving to solar still face banks that require guarantees — [Tunisie Numérique](https://www.tunisienumerique.com/transition-energetique-la-revolution-silencieuse-des-pme-tunisiennes/)
- Financing models for PV self-production in Tunisia, including leasing, have been studied — [energypedia](https://old.energypedia.info/wiki/Financing_Models_for_PV_in_Tunisia)

### Inferences
- C&I solar leasing is capital-intensive; the adaptation is to originate and operate projects while a leasing company or development lender holds the asset. - Water-purifier subscription suits Tunisia's tap-water distrust and bottled-water spend, with modest capital per customer and dinar-only revenue. Imported filters create FX exposure on the cost side.

### Gaps

## 13. Notable failures and capital-intensive traps

### Takeaway
The documented failures share one cause: thin-margin physical operations (inventory, fleets, warehouses, couriers) funded by venture capital in markets too small or too poor to reach scale. Survivors cut geography, moved to business customers or added financial services.

### Cited Findings
- Jumia announced in October 2024 that it would leave Tunisia and South Africa by year-end to focus on stronger markets — [Jumia](https://group.jumia.com/news/african-online-retailer-jumia-to-exit-south-africa-and-tunisia)
- Jumia said the two countries together were a small share of the group (headline cites 3.5% of orders and 3.0% of GMV) with low profitability — [Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/jumia-says-south-africa-and-tunisia-had-low-profitability-with-only-3-5-of-orders-3-0-of-gmv/)
- Jumia's own account of why it left — [Condia](https://thecondia.com/?p=18301)
- Swvl reported FY2025 revenue of $24.2M (up 41%) and net income of $1.3M versus a $10.3M loss in FY2024, driven by business customers and Gulf expansion (company release) — [Swvl](https://www.swvl.com/article/swvl-announces-fy-2025-results-revenue-up-41-to-24-2-million-net-income-1-3-million-ndr-of-128-gcc-revenue-up-122)
- Swvl extended its profit streak to three quarters by Q3 2025 — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/10/28/egypts-swvl-extends-profit-streak-to-three-quarters-on-gcc-expansion/)
- Glovo Tunisia is active: its country manager was profiled in November 2025 describing a move from delivery to e-commerce — [Managers](https://managers.tn/2025/11/06/adrian-mayans-directeur-general-de-glovo-tunisie-dun-simple-service-de-livraison-a-un-acteur-cle-du-e-commerce/)
- Glovo promoted quick commerce at Tunisia Retail Days in June 2026 — [Managers](https://managers.tn/2026/06/22/q-commerce-en-tunisie-glovo-appelle-lindustrie-du-retail-a-accelerer-lors-des-tunisia-retail-days-2026/)
- Breadfast passed $150M in annualised revenue by October 2024 despite Egypt's devaluations — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2024/10/22/quick-commerce-disruptor-breadfast-races-past-150m-arr-as-egypts-economy-falters)
- Rabbit expanded from Egypt into Saudi Arabia in 2025 — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2603775/business-economy)
- MaxAB-Wasoko closed its Morocco e-commerce operation in 2025 (see section 1) — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/07/11/maxab-wasoko-winds-down-e-commerce-in-morocco-as-fintech-becomes-core/)
- Vezeeta's investor value fell more than 80% (see section 4) — [LaunchBase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/04/24/78-below-cost-the-unforgiving-math-behind-digital-health-startup-vezeetas-pivot-to-bricks-and-mortar/)

### Inferences
- Rules for a Tunisia strategist drawn from these cases:
  1. Do not hold inventory or fleets financed by equity in a 12-million-person market. Jumia could not make Tunisia pay with a continental platform behind it.
  2. Prefer revenue in foreign currency (services to diaspora, foreign patients, foreign employers) or revenue forced by regulation (e-invoicing).
  3. If the model ends in lending or payments, the licence is the business; plan for it from day one or partner with a bank.
  4. Quick commerce survives only inside a dense, high-income base (Cairo) or inside a larger delivery platform (Glovo). It is not a standalone opportunity in Tunisia.
  5. Business customers pay; consumer transport and consumer marketplaces did not (Swvl's recovery came from B2B contracts).
- Swvl's "profit" is small and self-reported, after a collapse from a $1.5B SPAC valuation; it is a survival story, not a success.

### Gaps
- The brief's premise that Glovo exited Tunisia was not supported; no source of any Glovo Tunisia shutdown was found. Glovo exits from other markets (e.g. Ghana, Nigeria) were not researched.
- Quick-commerce shutdowns elsewhere (Egypt's earlier casualties, Turkey's Getir retrenchment) were not retrieved.
- Capital consumed by each failure (total funding burned) was not compiled.
- Tunisian startup failures were not researched.
